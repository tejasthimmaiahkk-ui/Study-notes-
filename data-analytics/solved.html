Q5 Two regression equations given: means, identification, r and σ 8 marks

Unit IISimultaneous equationsProperties of byx, bxy

The regression equations of two variables are 3X + 12Y = 19 and 9X + 3Y = 46. Find (a) the means of X and Y, (b) which equation is Y on X, (c) r, (d) σy if the variance of X is 9.

Solve simultaneously for the means. Assume one line is Y on X, check byx·bxy ≤ 1. Then byx = rσy/σx.

Show full step-by-step solution
  1. Means: from 9X + 3Y = 46, Y = (46 − 9X)/3. Substitute: 3X + 4(46 − 9X) = 19 → 3X + 184 − 36X = 19 → 33X = 165 → X̄ = 5; Ȳ = (46 − 45)/3 = 1/3.
  2. Assume 3X + 12Y = 19 is Y on X: Y = 19/12 − X/4 → byx = −1/4. Then 9X + 3Y = 46 is X on Y: X = 46/9 − Y/3 → bxy = −1/3.
  3. Check: byx·bxy = 1/12 ≈ 0.083 ≤ 1 ✓ — assumption is correct. (The other way round would give (−4)(−3) = 12 > 1, impossible.)
  4. r = −√(1/12) = −0.289 (negative because both coefficients are negative).
  5. σy: σx = 3. byx = r σy/σx → −0.25 = −0.2887 × σy/3 → σy = 0.75/0.2887 = 2.60.

X̄ = 5, Ȳ = 1/3; 3X + 12Y = 19 is Y on X; r = −0.289; σy ≈ 2.60.

Q8 Probability: addition, conditional, independence and combinations 10 marks

Unit IIIClassical probabilitynCrConditionalIndependence

(a) Two cards are drawn without replacement from a pack of 52. Find P(both kings), P(at least one king) and P(second is a king | first is a king).
(b) P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B), P(A | B), P(neither) and test whether A and B are independent.
(c) A box has 5 defective and 15 good bulbs. Three are drawn at random. Find P(exactly one defective).

(a) multiplication rule for dependent events / complement; (b) rearrange the addition theorem; (c) combinations favourable ÷ total.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) P(both kings) = 4/52 × 3/51 = 12/2652 = 1/221 ≈ 0.0045.
P(at least one king) = 1 − P(no king) = 1 − (48/52)(47/51) = 1 − 2256/2652 = 396/2652 = 33/221 ≈ 0.149.
P(2nd king | 1st king) = 3/51 = 1/17 (one king and one card are gone).

(b) P(A ∩ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∪ B) = 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.7 = 0.2.
P(A | B) = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4. P(neither) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 0.3.
P(A)·P(B) = 0.2 = P(A ∩ B) → A and B are independent (also P(A | B) = P(A)).

(c) Total ways = ²⁰C₃ = 1140. Favourable = ⁵C₁ × ¹⁵C₂ = 5 × 105 = 525. P = 525/1140 = 0.461.

1/221, 33/221, 1/17; 0.2, 0.4, 0.3, independent; 0.461.

Q14 One-sample t-test with confidence interval (and one- vs two-tailed) 10 marks

Unit IIIt-testConfidence intervalTails

Heights (inches) of 10 randomly chosen students: 63, 63, 64, 65, 66, 69, 69, 70, 70, 71. (a) Test at 5 % whether the mean height of the population is 65 inches. (b) Would the conclusion change if the claim to be tested were that the mean is more than 65? (c) Find the 95 % confidence interval for the mean.

Small sample, σ unknown → t with n − 1 = 9 df. Two-tailed critical value 2.262, one-tailed 1.833.

Show full step-by-step solution

x̄ = 670/10 = 67. Deviations: −4, −4, −3, −2, −1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4 → Σ(x − x̄)² = 16 + 16 + 9 + 4 + 1 + 4 + 4 + 9 + 9 + 16 = 88.
S = √(88/9) = √9.778 = 3.127.

(a) H₀: μ = 65; H₁: μ ≠ 65. t = (67 − 65)/(3.127/√10) = 2/0.9888 = 2.02. Table t0.05, 9 (two-tailed) = 2.262. 2.02 < 2.262 → accept H₀: the mean can be taken as 65.

(b) H₁: μ > 65 (one-tailed). Table t0.05, 9 (one-tailed) = 1.833. 2.02 > 1.833 → reject H₀: the mean height is significantly greater than 65. The conclusion changes because all 5 % of the rejection region is now in one tail.

(c) 95 % CI = x̄ ± t × S/√n = 67 ± 2.262 × 0.9888 = 67 ± 2.24 → 64.76 to 69.24. (It contains 65, consistent with (a).)

t = 2.02; two-tailed: accept; one-tailed: reject; CI 64.76–69.24.

Using √n with the population-style SD (divide by n) without adjusting; choosing the wrong tail; forgetting to state H₁.
