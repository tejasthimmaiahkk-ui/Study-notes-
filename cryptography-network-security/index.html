Cryptography & Network Security

From “what is a secret?” to how your browser sets up a secure HTTPS connection. The mathematics is taught from scratch, every cipher is worked out by hand, and most algorithms have a live demo you can experiment with.

Exam: 3 hoursMax marks 100 (Exam 80 + IA 20)56 teaching hours

Units

Unit I · 14 hours

Fundamentals & Mathematical Foundations

Security goals (CIA), attacks, X.800 services and mechanisms, cryptographic methods, integer arithmetic, GCD & extended Euclid, modular arithmetic, inverses, matrices, linear congruence.
Unit II · 14 hours

Symmetric Key Cryptography

Caesar, affine, Playfair, Vigenère, Hill, one-time pad, transposition, stream vs block ciphers, DES (round-by-round), Triple DES, AES and its four operations, key expansion, AES performance.
Unit III · 14 hours

Public Key Cryptography & Hashing

Modes of operation, modern stream ciphers, primes, primality tests, factorization, CRT, RSA, Rabin, ElGamal, elliptic curves, hash functions, MD family, Whirlpool, SHA-512.
Unit IV · 14 hours

Signatures, Key Management & Network Security

Digital signature schemes, KDC, Needham–Schroeder, Diffie–Hellman, certificates & PKI, Kerberos, PGP, S/MIME, SSL/TLS handshake, IPSec (AH/ESP), IKE.
Exam kit

Revision & Exam Kit

One-page summaries, comparison tables, formula sheet, likely 2/5/10-mark questions and a mixed self-test.

How to score well in this paper

  • Always draw the block diagram (DES round, AES round, Kerberos, SSL handshake, IPSec modes). Diagrams fetch marks quickly.
  • Use the four-part structure: definition → working → example → advantages/limitations.
  • For comparison questions (DES vs AES, AH vs ESP, PGP vs S/MIME, stream vs block) answer in a table.
  • Show the extended Euclidean table — examiners award marks per row.
  • For RSA: write n, φ(n), e, d and verify e·d mod φ(n) = 1.
  • For classical ciphers: write the letter → number mapping first, then the formula, then the table of results.
  • For modular powers, show square-and-multiply steps instead of a single final number.
