1.0 Foundation: fast and accurate calculation

Aptitude questions are timed. Most mistakes come from slow or careless arithmetic, not from not knowing the method. Spend time here first — it pays off in every later topic.

Order of operations: BODMAS

Evaluate in this order: Brackets → Orders/of (powers, roots, "of") → Division and Multiplication (left to right) → Addition and Subtraction (left to right).
Simplification questions; every formula you substitute into.
18 ÷ 3 × 2 + 4² − (6 − 2) = 18 ÷ 3 × 2 + 16 − 4 = 6 × 2 + 16 − 4 = 12 + 16 − 4 = 24. "of" means multiply: ½ of 40 + 5 = 25.
Division and multiplication have equal priority — go left to right (18 ÷ 3 × 2 = 12, not 3).

Mental-maths shortcuts

Rewrite awkward numbers into easy ones.
Every calculation-heavy question.

1.1 Number system

Classification of numbers

  • Natural numbers N = {1, 2, 3, …} — counting numbers.
  • Whole numbers W = {0, 1, 2, …} — naturals plus zero.
  • Integers Z = {…, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …}.
  • Rational numbers Q — can be written p/q (q ≠ 0): ¾, −5, 0.25, 0.333… (terminating or repeating decimals).
  • Irrational numbers — cannot be written as p/q; non-terminating, non-repeating: √2, π.
  • Real numbers R — all rationals and irrationals.
  • Even (divisible by 2) / odd; prime (exactly two factors: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …; 2 is the only even prime) / composite (more than two factors); 1 is neither; co-primes have HCF 1 (8 and 15).
Questions like "which is a rational number", "how many primes between 50 and 100" (there are 10: 53, 59, 61, 67, 71, 73, 79, 83, 89, 97).
Place value vs face value: in 58,736 the face value of 7 is 7, its place value is 700.
Useful sums: 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1)/2; 1² + … + n² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6; 1³ + … + n³ = [n(n + 1)/2]². Sum of first n odd numbers = n²; first n even numbers = n(n + 1).
Number of factors of N = ap × bq × cr (prime factorisation) is (p + 1)(q + 1)(r + 1). 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5 → 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 factors.
0 is even; 1 is not prime; negative numbers are not prime.
  1. Find the sum of the first 20 natural numbers and of their squares.
    Show answer
    20 × 21/2 = 210; 20 × 21 × 41/6 = 2870.
  2. How many factors does 720 have?
    Show answer
    720 = 2⁴ × 3² × 5 → 5 × 3 × 2 = 30.
  3. Difference between the place value and face value of 6 in 46,329.
    Show answer
    6000 − 6 = 5994.

1.3 LCM and HCF

Definitions

HCF (Highest Common Factor, also GCD) of two or more numbers is the largest number that divides all of them exactly. LCM (Least Common Multiple) is the smallest number that is exactly divisible by all of them.
HCF → "largest", "maximum size", "greatest number that divides", cutting into equal pieces, tiles, groups. LCM → "least", "together again", "bells ring together", "minimum number divisible by".

Prime factorisation method: HCF = product of common primes with the lowest powers; LCM = product of all primes with the highest powers.

36 = 2² × 3², 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 → HCF = 2² × 3 = 12; LCM = 2² × 3² × 7 = 252.

Division method for HCF (Euclid): divide the larger by the smaller, then the divisor by the remainder, until remainder 0; the last divisor is the HCF. HCF(1071, 462): 1071 = 2 × 462 + 147; 462 = 3 × 147 + 21; 147 = 7 × 21 + 0 → 21.

Common-division method for LCM: divide all numbers by common primes repeatedly; LCM = product of divisors and final quotients.

HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers HCF of fractions = HCF of numeratorsLCM of denominators    LCM of fractions = LCM of numeratorsHCF of denominators
HCF × LCM = product holds only for two numbers. HCF always divides the LCM.
  1. Check: 12 × 252 = 3024 = 36 × 84 ✓.
  2. Product of two numbers is 2028, HCF 13. LCM? 2028/13 = 156.
  3. Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes. They ring together at 9:00. When next? LCM(6, 8, 12) = 24 → 9:24.
  4. Least number which when divided by 12, 15, 20 leaves remainder 4 in each case: LCM = 60 → 60 + 4 = 64.
  5. Greatest number that divides 43, 91 and 183 leaving the same remainder: HCF of the differences: 91 − 43 = 48, 183 − 91 = 92, 183 − 43 = 140 → HCF(48, 92, 140) = 4.
  6. Largest square tile for a floor 15 m 17 cm × 9 m 2 cm, and how many tiles? HCF(1517, 902) = 41 cm; tiles = (1517/41) × (902/41) = 37 × 22 = 814.
  7. HCF and LCM of fractions 2/3, 8/9, 16/81, 10/27: HCF = HCF(2, 8, 16, 10)/LCM(3, 9, 81, 27) = 2/81. LCM of 1/3, 5/6, 2/9 = LCM(1, 5, 2)/HCF(3, 6, 9) = 10/3.
  1. Find the HCF and LCM of 48, 72 and 108.
    Show answer
    48 = 2⁴·3, 72 = 2³·3², 108 = 2²·3³ → HCF = 2²·3 = 12; LCM = 2⁴·3³ = 432.
  2. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 693. One number is 77. Find the other.
    Show answer
    11 × 693/77 = 99.
  3. Find the least number which when divided by 5, 6, 7 and 8 leaves remainder 3 but is divisible by 9.
    Show answer
    Numbers = 840k + 3. Need divisible by 9: 840 ≡ 3 (mod 9), so 3k + 3 ≡ 0 → k ≡ 2 (mod 3). k = 2 → 1683 (1683 = 9 × 187).
  4. Four runners complete a lap in 200, 300, 360 and 450 seconds. After how long do they meet at the start again?
    Show answer
    LCM = 1800 s = 30 minutes.

1.4 Remainders (basic concepts)

The division relation and remainder rules

Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder, with 0 ≤ remainder < divisor. Remainders can be added and multiplied: the remainder of a sum/product equals the remainder of the sum/product of the individual remainders.
"Find the remainder when…", unit-digit questions, calendar/day-of-week problems, cyclic patterns.
  • Product rule: (17 × 23 × 31) ÷ 5 → remainders 2, 3, 1 → 2 × 3 × 1 = 6 → 6 ÷ 5 → remainder 1.
  • Divisor-of-divisor rule: a number leaves remainder 36 when divided by 136. Remainder when divided by 17 (a factor of 136)? 36 ÷ 17 → 2.
  • Negative remainder: 25 ≡ −1 (mod 26) → 2525 ≡ (−1)25 = −1 → remainder 26 − 1 = 25.
  • Power cycles: 2100 ÷ 7: 2³ = 8 ≡ 1 (mod 7) → 299 = (2³)33 ≡ 1 → 2100 ≡ 2 → remainder 2.
Remainders cannot be divided directly; always reduce the final result below the divisor.

Unit digit and cyclicity

The unit digits of powers repeat in short cycles: 2 → 2, 4, 8, 6 (cycle 4); 3 → 3, 9, 7, 1; 7 → 7, 9, 3, 1; 8 → 8, 4, 2, 6 (all cycle 4); 4 → 4, 6 and 9 → 9, 1 (cycle 2); 0, 1, 5, 6 always repeat themselves.
"Find the unit digit of 795 × 358."
Divide the exponent by the cycle length (4) and use the remainder (remainder 0 → use the 4th term). 795: 95 mod 4 = 3 → 3rd term → 3. 365 × 659 × 771: 3, 6, 3 → 3 × 6 × 3 = 54 → 4.
Only the last digit of the base matters; the exponent must be reduced mod 4, not mod 10.
  1. Remainder when 1234 × 1235 × 1236 is divided by 12.
    Show answer
    1234 ≡ 10, 1235 ≡ 11, 1236 ≡ 0 (mod 12) → product ≡ 0.
  2. Unit digit of 251 + 352.
    Show answer
    51 mod 4 = 3 → 8; 52 mod 4 = 0 → 1; 8 + 1 = 9.
  3. Remainder when 321 is divided by 5.
    Show answer
    3⁴ = 81 ≡ 1 (mod 5); 321 = (3⁴)⁵ × 3 ≡ 3.
  4. A number divided by 5 leaves remainder 3. What is the remainder when its square is divided by 5?
    Show answer
    3² = 9 → 4.

1.5 Percentages and applications

Meaning and conversions

Per cent means "per hundred": x % = x/100. A percentage compares a quantity with 100, so different totals can be compared fairly (45/60 and 70/100 → 75 % vs 70 %).
Marks, discounts, interest, growth rates, GST, election results, data interpretation — percentages are everywhere.

Fraction → %: multiply by 100. % → fraction: divide by 100. x % of y = y % of x (8 % of 50 = 50 % of 8 = 4 — use whichever is easier).

Percentage change and comparison

% change = new − oldold × 100     A is what % of B = AB × 100 If A is r % more than B, then B is less than A by r100 + r × 100 %. (If A is r % less than B, B is more than A by r/(100 − r) × 100 %.)
Salary comparisons, price rise vs consumption, growth of sales.
A's salary is 25 % more than B's → B's is less than A's by 25/125 × 100 = 20 %. Price of sugar rises 25 % → to keep expenditure unchanged, reduce consumption by 25/125 × 100 = 20 %.
Always identify the base (the "of" or "than" quantity) — it changes the answer.

Successive percentage change

Two successive changes of a % and b % equal a single change of a + b + ab100 %    (use negative signs for decreases)
Price increased twice, successive discounts, area when length and breadth change, population growth over years.
+10 % then +20 % → 10 + 20 + 2 = +32 %. +20 % then −20 % → 20 − 20 − 4 = −4 %. Length +20 %, breadth −10 % → area changes 20 − 10 − 2 = +8 %.
Growth/depreciation for n years: P(1 ± r/100)n. Population 10,000 growing 10 % a year → after 2 years 10,000 × 1.1² = 12,100. Machine worth ₹50,000 depreciating 10 % a year → after 2 years 50,000 × 0.9² = ₹40,500.
The order of the changes does not matter (multiplication is commutative), but the base does.
  1. A candidate scoring 30 % fails by 30 marks; another scoring 40 % gets 30 marks more than the pass mark. Find the maximum marks and pass mark. The difference of 10 % = 30 + 30 = 60 marks → 100 % = 600. Pass mark = 30 % of 600 + 30 = 210.
  2. In an election between two candidates, the winner got 60 % of the valid votes and won by 200 votes. Total valid votes? 60 % − 40 % = 20 % = 200 → 1000.
  3. After spending 80 % of his salary, a man saves ₹6,000. Salary? 20 % = 6,000 → ₹30,000.
  1. If the price of petrol rises by 20 %, by what % must a family cut consumption to keep spending the same?
    Show answer
    20/120 × 100 = 16⅔ %.
  2. A number is increased by 20 % and then decreased by 25 %. Net change?
    Show answer
    20 − 25 − 5 = −10 % (decrease of 10 %).
  3. The population of a town is 1,25,000. It grows 4 % in the first year and 5 % in the second. Population after 2 years?
    Show answer
    1,25,000 × 1.04 × 1.05 = 1,36,500.
  4. What percentage of 3/5 is 1/5?
    Show answer
    (1/5)/(3/5) × 100 = 33⅓ %.

1.6 Profit, loss and discount

Basic terms and formulas

Cost price (CP) = price paid by the seller. Selling price (SP) = price received. Marked price (MP / list price) = price printed on the tag. Discount is always on MP; profit/loss % is always on CP (unless stated otherwise).
Retail pricing, sales offers, business decisions, dishonest-dealer puzzles.
Profit = SP − CP,   Profit % = ProfitCP × 100    Loss % = LossCP × 100 SP = CP × 100 + P%100    CP = SP × 100100 + P%    (replace + P by − L for loss)
Discount = MP − SP,   Discount % = DiscountMP × 100,   SP = MP × 100 − D%100 MPCP = 100 + P%100 − D%    successive discounts d₁, d₂: single discount = d₁ + d₂ − d₁d₂/100
Mixing bases (taking profit on SP or discount on CP) is the most common error.
  1. CP ₹400, SP ₹500. Profit = 100 → 100/400 × 100 = 25 %.
  2. Selling at ₹540 causes a 10 % loss. What SP gives a 15 % gain? CP = 540 × 100/90 = 600 → SP = 600 × 115/100 = ₹690.
  3. Two articles are sold at ₹990 each, one at 10 % gain and the other at 10 % loss. Overall? Always a loss of (x/10)² = 1 %. Check: CPs 900 and 1100 = 2000; SP 1980 → loss 20 = 1 %.
  4. A dishonest dealer sells at cost price but uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg. Gain % = (1000 − 900)/900 × 100 = 11.11 %.
  5. Marked 40 % above CP, discount 10 %. MP/CP = 1.4; SP = 1.4 × 0.9 = 1.26 CP → 26 % profit.
  6. Successive discounts of 20 % and 10% = 20 + 10 − 2 = 28 % (not 30 %).
  7. "Buy 4 get 1 free" → pay for 4, get 5 → discount = 1/5 = 20 %.
  8. By selling 33 m of cloth a shopkeeper gains the SP of 11 m. Gain %? SP(33) − CP(33) = SP(11) → CP(33) = SP(22) → gain = 11/22 = 50 %.
  1. An article bought for ₹1,200 is sold at a loss of 15 %. Find the SP.
    Show answer
    1,200 × 85/100 = ₹1,020.
  2. A shopkeeper allows 20 % discount on MP and still gains 20 %. If CP is ₹800, find MP.
    Show answer
    SP = 960 = 0.8 MP → MP = ₹1,200. (Or MP = CP × 120/80.)
  3. The CP of 20 articles equals the SP of 16 articles. Profit %?
    Show answer
    (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = 25 %.
  4. Find a single discount equivalent to successive discounts of 10 %, 20 % and 25 %.
    Show answer
    1 − 0.9 × 0.8 × 0.75 = 1 − 0.54 = 46 %.

1.7 Ratio, proportion and partnership

Ratio

A ratio a : b compares two quantities of the same kind by division (a/b). a is the antecedent, b the consequent. Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number does not change a ratio (6 : 9 = 2 : 3).
Sharing money, mixing ingredients, ages, partnerships, maps and scales.
  • Combining ratios: A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5 → make B equal (LCM of 3 and 4 = 12): A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.
  • Dividing a quantity in a : b: shares are a/(a + b) and b/(a + b) of the total. ₹1,200 in 3 : 5 → 450 and 750.
  • Compound ratio of a:b and c:d is ac : bd (2:3, 4:5, 6:7 → 48 : 105 = 16 : 35). Duplicate a² : b², triplicate a³ : b³, sub-duplicate √a : √b (16 : 25 → 4 : 5).
  • Comparing ratios: 3/5 vs 4/7 → cross-multiply 21 vs 20 → 3/5 is larger.
Quantities must be in the same unit before forming a ratio (50 paise : ₹2 = 1 : 4).

Proportion; direct and inverse proportion

Four numbers are in proportion (a : b :: c : d) if a/b = c/d, i.e. a × d = b × c (product of extremes = product of means). In direct proportion two quantities rise and fall together (y = kx); in inverse proportion one rises as the other falls (xy = k).
Unitary method problems: cost of items (direct), workers and days (inverse), speed and time (inverse).
  • Fourth proportional to a, b, c = bc/a: to 4, 9, 12 → 27.
  • Third proportional to a, b = b²/a: to 9, 12 → 16.
  • Mean proportional between a and b = √(ab): between 4 and 25 → 10.
  • Direct: 12 pens cost ₹90 → 20 pens cost 90 × 20/12 = ₹150.
  • Inverse: 12 men finish in 15 days → 18 men take 12 × 15/18 = 10 days.
Decide first whether the relation is direct or inverse — reversing it gives a wrong answer.

Present ages of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5. After 5 years the ratio will be 5 : 6. Find their present ages.

  1. Let ages be 4x and 5x.
  2. (4x + 5)/(5x + 5) = 5/6 → 24x + 30 = 25x + 25 → x = 5.
  3. Ages: 20 and 25 years.

Partnership

When two or more people invest in a business, profit (or loss) is shared in the ratio of their capital × time.
  • Simple partnership: capitals invested for the same period → share in ratio of capitals.
  • Compound partnership: different periods → ratio of (capital × months).
  • A working (active) partner manages the business and may first receive a salary/commission; a sleeping partner only invests.
Dividing business profits fairly; questions with partners joining or leaving mid-year.
  1. A invests ₹50,000 for the whole year; B joins after 4 months with ₹60,000. Profit ₹27,000. Ratio = 50,000 × 12 : 60,000 × 8 = 6,00,000 : 4,80,000 = 5 : 4 → A gets ₹15,000, B ₹12,000.
  2. A, B, C invest ₹20,000, ₹30,000, ₹50,000. A is the working partner and gets 10 % of profit as salary. Profit ₹50,000. Salary = 5,000; remaining 45,000 in 2 : 3 : 5 → 9,000, 13,500, 22,500. A's total = ₹14,000.
If capital changes during the year, split each partner's investment into periods and add capital × months for each period.

When two items with prices c (cheaper) and d (dearer) are mixed to get a mean price m, the quantities are in the ratio (d − m) : (m − c). Rice at ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg mixed to sell at ₹45/kg (at cost) → (60 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 3 : 1.

  1. If a : b = 3 : 4 and b : c = 6 : 7, find a : b : c and a : c.
    Show answer
    Make b = 12: a : b = 9 : 12, b : c = 12 : 14 → 9 : 12 : 14; a : c = 9 : 14.
  2. Divide ₹7,800 among A, B, C in the ratio ½ : ⅓ : ¼.
    Show answer
    Multiply by 12: 6 : 4 : 3 (sum 13) → ₹3,600, ₹2,400, ₹1,800.
  3. A and B start a business with ₹15,000 and ₹20,000. After 6 months A adds ₹5,000. Share of B in a yearly profit of ₹11,000?
    Show answer
    A: 15,000 × 6 + 20,000 × 6 = 2,10,000; B: 20,000 × 12 = 2,40,000 → 7 : 8 → B = 11,000 × 8/15 = ₹5,866.67.
  4. Find the mean proportional between 0.08 and 0.18.
    Show answer
    √(0.0144) = 0.12.

1.8 Averages

Simple and weighted average

Average (arithmetic mean) = sum of observations ÷ number of observations. A weighted average gives each value an importance (weight): (w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + …)/(w₁ + w₂ + …).
Average marks, run rate, average salary of a combined group, average price per kg of a mixture.

Properties: adding (subtracting/multiplying) a constant to every value adds (subtracts/multiplies) the average by the same constant. Sum = average × number — the most-used rearrangement.

Combined group: Section A has 40 students averaging 60, B has 60 averaging 70 → (40 × 60 + 60 × 70)/100 = 66.

Consecutive numbers: average of an AP = (first + last)/2; average of first n natural numbers = (n + 1)/2.

Averages of averages are wrong unless group sizes are equal — use weights.
  1. The average of 10 numbers is 25. If 40 is replaced by 20, new average? Sum falls by 20 → average falls by 20/10 = 2 → 23.
  2. The average age of 30 students is 15. Including the teacher, it becomes 16. Teacher's age? 16 × 31 − 15 × 30 = 496 − 450 = 46. Shortcut: 15 + 31 × 1 = 46.
  3. A batsman averages 40 in 10 innings. What must he score in the 11th to raise his average to 42? 42 × 11 − 40 × 10 = 462 − 400 = 62.
  4. Average speed: going at 60 km/h and returning the same distance at 40 km/h → 2 × 60 × 40/(60 + 40) = 48 km/h (not 50 — equal distances, not equal times).
  1. The average of 5 consecutive odd numbers is 61. Find the largest.
    Show answer
    Middle = 61 → numbers 57, 59, 61, 63, 65 → 65.
  2. The average weight of 8 people increases by 2.5 kg when a new person replaces one weighing 65 kg. Weight of the new person?
    Show answer
    65 + 8 × 2.5 = 85 kg.
  3. The average salary of 20 workers is ₹15,000; of 5 managers ₹40,000. Average of all?
    Show answer
    (3,00,000 + 2,00,000)/25 = ₹20,000.

1.9 Time, speed and distance

Basic concepts

Speed = Distance ÷ Time, so Distance = Speed × Time and Time = Distance ÷ Speed. For a fixed distance, speed and time are inversely proportional; for a fixed time, distance ∝ speed.
Travel planning, trains, races, boats, catching up, meeting problems.

Unit conversion: km/h → m/s multiply by 5/18; m/s → km/h multiply by 18/5. (72 km/h = 20 m/s; 15 m/s = 54 km/h.)

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. For equal distances at x and y: 2xy/(x + y).

Speed ratio a : b → time ratio b : a for the same distance.

Always convert to consistent units before substituting; average speed is NOT the average of speeds (unless equal times).

Relative speed

When two bodies move: same direction → relative speed = difference of speeds; opposite directions → relative speed = sum. Then time to meet/cross = distance between them ÷ relative speed.
Two trains crossing, a police car catching a thief, two people walking towards each other.
Two people 20 km apart walk towards each other at 4 and 6 km/h → meet after 20/(4 + 6) = 2 h. A thief runs at 8 km/h; police start 1 km behind at 10 km/h → caught after 1/(10 − 8) = 0.5 h, having run 5 km.
Relative speed applies to the gap between the bodies — use the right starting distance.

Problems on trains

A train has length, so crossing means the whole train must pass. Distance to cover:
  • crossing a pole / man standing / signal → length of train;
  • crossing a platform / bridge / tunnel → train length + platform length;
  • crossing another train → sum of both lengths, at relative speed;
  • passing a moving man → train length at relative speed.
A favourite in every aptitude test (railway exams, campus placement).
  1. A 150 m train at 54 km/h crosses a pole in? 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s → 150/15 = 10 s. A 300 m platform? (150 + 300)/15 = 30 s.
  2. Trains 120 m and 180 m at 50 and 40 km/h, opposite directions: relative 90 km/h = 25 m/s → 300/25 = 12 s. Same direction: relative 10 km/h = 25/9 m/s → 300 × 9/25 = 108 s.
  3. A 125 m train at 50 km/h passes a man walking at 5 km/h in the same direction: relative 45 km/h = 12.5 m/s → 10 s.
  4. A train crosses a pole in 15 s and a 100 m platform in 25 s. Length and speed? Extra 100 m takes 10 s → speed 10 m/s = 36 km/h; length = 10 × 15 = 150 m.
Forgetting to add the train's own length is the classic mistake.

Boat speed in still water u, stream v: downstream = u + v, upstream = u − v; u = (down + up)/2, v = (down − up)/2. A boat at 10 km/h in a 2 km/h stream goes 24 km down and back in 24/12 + 24/8 = 5 h. In races, "A beats B by x m" means when A finishes, B is x m behind.

  1. Walking at 5 km/h a man reaches 10 minutes early; at 4 km/h he is 5 minutes late. Distance?
    Show answer
    Time difference = 15 min: d/4 − d/5 = 1/4 h → d/20 = 1/4 → 5 km.
  2. A car covers half a journey at 40 km/h and the other half at 60 km/h. Average speed?
    Show answer
    2 × 40 × 60/100 = 48 km/h.
  3. A 240 m train passes a 360 m bridge in 30 s. Speed in km/h?
    Show answer
    600/30 = 20 m/s = 72 km/h.
  4. Two trains of 100 m each pass in opposite directions in 8 s. One is twice as fast as the other. Speeds?
    Show answer
    Relative = 200/8 = 25 m/s = 3x → x = 25/3 m/s = 30 km/h and 60 km/h.

1.10 Time and work

Work, rate and efficiency

If A finishes a job in n days, A does 1/n of the job per day (A's rate or efficiency). Rates of people working together add up: time together = 1 ÷ (sum of rates). Work = rate × time.
Project scheduling, how many workers to hire, sharing wages, machines producing output.

LCM (total-units) method — avoids fractions:

  1. Take total work = LCM of the individual days.
  2. Each person's units/day = total ÷ their days.
  3. Add/subtract rates as required; time = total ÷ combined rate.

Example: A takes 10 days, B 15 days. Total = 30 units; A = 3/day, B = 2/day → together 5/day → 6 days.

M₁ × D₁ × H₁W₁ = M₂ × D₂ × H₂W₂

(M = men, D = days, H = hours/day, W = work)

Wages are shared in the ratio of work done (= ratio of efficiencies when working the same time).

Assumes everyone works at a constant rate and the work is divisible.
  1. A and B together finish in 12 days; A alone in 20 days. B alone? Total 60: A + B = 5/day, A = 3 → B = 2 → 30 days.
  2. A, B, C take 10, 12, 15 days. All together? Total 60; rates 6 + 5 + 4 = 15 → 4 days.
  3. A is twice as efficient as B; together they finish in 14 days. A alone? Rates 2 : 1 → work = 3 × 14 = 42 units → A alone 42/2 = 21 days.
  4. 12 men build a wall in 18 days working 8 h/day. How many days for 16 men working 9 h/day? 12 × 18 × 8 = 16 × D × 9 → D = 12 days.
  5. A can do a job in 6 days, B in 8. Together they earn ₹1,400. Rates (LCM 24): 4 : 3 → A ₹800, B ₹600.
  6. A (8 days) and B (12 days) work on alternate days, A starting. Total 24; A 3, B 2 → every 2 days 5 units. 4 cycles = 20 units in 8 days; day 9: A does 3 (23 done); day 10: B needs 1 unit = half a day → 9.5 days.
  1. A can do a piece of work in 20 days and B in 30 days. They work together for 6 days, then A leaves. In how many more days will B finish?
    Show answer
    Total 60; A 3, B 2 → 6 days × 5 = 30 done; remaining 30 at 2/day = 15 days.
  2. 10 men can complete a job in 15 days. After 5 days, 5 more men join. Total time taken?
    Show answer
    Work = 150 man-days; 5 days × 10 = 50 done; 100 left with 15 men = 6⅔ days → total 11⅔ days.
  3. A does half as much work as B in three-fourths of the time. If together they take 18 days, how long does B take alone?
    Show answer
    In time t, B does 1 unit; A does ½ unit in ¾t → A's rate = (½)/(¾t) = 2/(3t), B's = 1/t → A : B = 2 : 3. Work = 5 × 18 = 90 units → B alone 90/3 = 30 days.

1.11 Pipes and cisterns

Same idea as time and work

A pipe that fills a tank in n hours adds 1/n of the tank per hour (an inlet, positive work); a pipe or leak that empties it in m hours removes 1/m per hour (an outlet, negative work). Net rate = sum of inlet rates − sum of outlet rates.
Water tanks, reservoirs, any process with inflow and outflow (bank balance, inventory).
  1. A fills in 20 min, B in 30 min, C empties in 15 min. All open? Total 60 units: A 3, B 2, C −4 → net 1/min → 60 min.
  2. A pipe fills a tank in 6 h; because of a leak it takes 7 h. The leak alone empties a full tank in? 1/6 − 1/7 = 1/42 → 42 h.
  3. Pipes A and B fill a tank in 12 and 15 minutes. Both are opened, and B is turned off after 3 minutes. Total time? Total 60: A 5, B 4. In 3 min: 27 units; remaining 33 by A at 5/min = 6.6 min → total 9.6 min.
If the net rate is negative the tank never fills (it empties instead).
  1. Two pipes fill a tank in 10 and 12 hours; a third empties it in 20 hours. All three open — time to fill?
    Show answer
    LCM 60: 6 + 5 − 3 = 8/h → 7.5 hours.
  2. A tap fills a bucket in 4 minutes; a hole empties it in 12 minutes. Time to fill with the hole open?
    Show answer
    1/4 − 1/12 = 1/6 → 6 minutes.

1.12 Algebra basics: linear and quadratic equations

A variable (x, y) stands for an unknown number. An expression combines variables and numbers (3x + 5). An equation says two expressions are equal (3x + 5 = 20); solving it means finding the value(s) that make it true. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Identities to know: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²; (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²; a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b); (a + b)³ = a³ + b³ + 3ab(a + b); a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² − ab + b²).

Linear equations in one variable

Of the form ax + b = 0 (highest power of x is 1) — exactly one solution x = −b/a.
Translating word problems: ages, numbers, money, consecutive integers.
  1. Remove brackets and fractions (multiply by the LCM of denominators).
  2. Bring x-terms to one side, numbers to the other.
  3. Divide by the coefficient of x. Check by substituting.
3x + 7 = 22 → 3x = 15 → x = 5. (2x − 1)/3 = (x + 2)/4 → 4(2x − 1) = 3(x + 2) → 8x − 4 = 3x + 6 → 5x = 10 → x = 2.
Multiplying by an expression that can be zero can create false solutions — always check.

Linear equations in two variables

Two unknowns need two independent equations. Graphically each is a straight line; the solution is their intersection point.
"3 pens and 2 pencils cost…", "the sum and difference of two numbers…".

Substitution: 2x + 3y = 12, 3x − y = 7 → y = 3x − 7 → 2x + 9x − 21 = 12 → x = 3, y = 2.

Elimination: x + y = 14, x − y = 4 → add: 2x = 18 → x = 9, y = 5.

Word problem: 3 pens + 2 pencils = ₹38; 2 pens + 3 pencils = ₹32. Add: 5(p + q) = 70 → p + q = 14. Subtract: p − q = 6 → pen = ₹10, pencil = ₹4.

Cross-multiplication (for a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0, a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0): x = (b₁c₂ − b₂c₁)/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁), y = (c₁a₂ − c₂a₁)/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁).

If a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ the lines are parallel → no solution; if all three ratios are equal → infinitely many solutions.

Quadratic equations

Of the form ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0) — at most two solutions (roots). The graph is a parabola; roots are where it cuts the x-axis.
Area problems ("length is 3 m more than breadth, area 40 m²"), product of consecutive numbers, profit maximisation, aptitude "compare the roots" questions.

1. Factorisation (splitting the middle term): find two numbers whose product = a·c and sum = b.

x² − 5x + 6 = 0: product 6, sum −5 → −2, −3 → (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 2, 3.
6x² − 7x − 3 = 0: a·c = −18, sum −7 → −9, +2 → 6x² − 9x + 2x − 3 = 3x(2x − 3) + 1(2x − 3) = (3x + 1)(2x − 3) → x = 3/2, −1/3.

x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a    Discriminant D = b² − 4ac

D > 0: two distinct real roots (rational if D is a perfect square) · D = 0: two equal roots (−b/2a) · D < 0: no real roots

Sum of roots α + β = −b/a    Product αβ = c/a    Equation with roots α, β: x² − (α + β)x + αβ = 0

x² − 4x + 1 = 0: D = 16 − 4 = 12 → x = (4 ± √12)/2 = 2 ± √3. Equation with roots 3 and −5: x² + 2x − 15 = 0.

Word problem: A rectangle's length is 3 m more than its breadth and its area is 40 m². x(x + 3) = 40 → x² + 3x − 40 = 0 → (x + 8)(x − 5) = 0 → breadth 5 m, length 8 m (reject −8).

Reject roots that make no sense in context (negative lengths, fractional people).
  1. The sum of three consecutive even numbers is 78. Find them.
    Show answer
    x + x + 2 + x + 4 = 78 → x = 24 → 24, 26, 28.
  2. A father is three times as old as his son. After 12 years he will be twice as old. Present ages?
    Show answer
    3x + 12 = 2(x + 12) → x = 12 → son 12, father 36.
  3. Solve 2x² + 5x − 3 = 0.
    Show answer
    a·c = −6, sum 5 → 6, −1 → (2x − 1)(x + 3) = 0 → x = ½, −3.
  4. Find k if x² − kx + 9 = 0 has equal roots.
    Show answer
    D = k² − 36 = 0 → k = ±6.
  5. The product of two consecutive positive integers is 132. Find them.
    Show answer
    x(x + 1) = 132 → x² + x − 132 = 0 → (x + 12)(x − 11) = 0 → 11 and 12.

1.13 Unit I review and quick test

  • HCF × LCM = product (two numbers); HCF ↔ "largest", LCM ↔ "least / together".
  • Remainders multiply; use cycles of 4 for unit digits.
  • Successive % = a + b + ab/100; r % more → r/(100 + r) less.
  • Profit % on CP, discount % on MP; MP/CP = (100 + P)/(100 − D).
  • Partnership: share ∝ capital × time.
  • Average speed (equal distances) = 2xy/(x + y); trains: add lengths; relative speed: same direction subtract, opposite add; ×5/18.
  • Work: LCM method; M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂; outlets are negative work.
  • Quadratic: factorise or (−b ± √D)/2a; sum −b/a, product c/a.
