Q1 HCF and LCM applications: remainders, traffic lights and pairs 10 marks

Unit IHCFLCMRemaindersHCF × LCM
  1. Find the greatest number that divides 1657 and 2037 leaving remainders 6 and 5 respectively.
  2. Find the least number which, when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 54, leaves a remainder of 8 in each case.
  3. Find the least number which leaves a remainder of 3 when divided by 5, 6, 7 or 8, but is exactly divisible by 9.
  4. Three traffic lights change every 48 s, 72 s and 108 s. They change together at 8:20:00 a.m. When will they next change together?
  5. The sum of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 27. Find all possible pairs of numbers.

Spot the key word: "greatest number that divides" → HCF; "least number divisible by" or "together again" → LCM. With a remainder, first subtract it (HCF) or add it (LCM). For (e) write the numbers as 27a and 27b with a, b co-prime.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Greatest number with different remainders

The required number divides 1657 − 6 = 1651 and 2037 − 5 = 2032 exactly, so it is HCF(1651, 2032).

Euclid's division: 2032 = 1 × 1651 + 381; 1651 = 4 × 381 + 127; 381 = 3 × 127 + 0. So HCF = 127.

Check: 1657 = 13 × 127 + 6 ✓, 2037 = 16 × 127 + 5 ✓.

(b) Least number with the same remainder

12 = 2²·3, 15 = 3·5, 20 = 2²·5, 54 = 2·3³. LCM = 2²·3³·5 = 540. Required number = 540 + 8 = 548.

(c) Same remainder and divisible by 9

LCM(5, 6, 7, 8) = 840, so the number is 840k + 3. 840 = 9 × 93 + 3, so 840k + 3 ≡ 3k + 3 (mod 9).

Need 3k + 3 divisible by 9 → k + 1 divisible by 3 → smallest k = 2. Number = 840 × 2 + 3 = 1683. Check: digit sum 1+6+8+3 = 18 → divisible by 9 ✓.

(d) Lights changing together

48 = 2⁴·3, 72 = 2³·3², 108 = 2²·3³. LCM = 2⁴·3³ = 432 s = 7 min 12 s. Next together at 8:27:12 a.m.

(e) Pairs with a given HCF and sum

Let the numbers be 27a and 27b with HCF(a, b) = 1. Then 27(a + b) = 216 → a + b = 8.

Co-prime pairs adding to 8: (1, 7) and (3, 5). [(2, 6) and (4, 4) are not co-prime.] So the pairs are (27, 189) and (81, 135).

(a) 127 (b) 548 (c) 1683 (d) 8:27:12 a.m. (e) (27, 189) and (81, 135)

Adding the remainder in an HCF question or subtracting it in an LCM question. In (e), forgetting to reject pairs like (2, 6), which would make the HCF 54, not 27.

Q2 Number system: remainders, unit digits, divisibility and factors 10 marks

Unit ICyclicityRemainder theoremDivisibility by 72Number of factors
  1. Find the remainder when 2¹⁰⁰ is divided by 7.
  2. Find the unit digit of 7⁹⁵ × 3⁵⁸.
  3. A number divided by 342 leaves remainder 47. What is the remainder when the same number is divided by 18?
  4. Find the digits x and y if the five-digit number 4x67y is divisible by 72.
  5. Find the number of factors of 720 and the sum of all its factors.
  6. Find the remainder when 17 × 23 × 29 is divided by 12.

Powers → find a power that gives remainder 1 (or the unit-digit cycle of 4). Products → replace each number by its remainder first. 72 = 8 × 9 with 8 and 9 co-prime, so test both rules. Factors → write the prime factorisation N = paqbrc.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) 2¹⁰⁰ mod 7

2³ = 8 = 7 + 1, so 2³ leaves remainder 1. 100 = 3 × 33 + 1, so 2¹⁰⁰ = (2³)³³ × 2 → remainder 1³³ × 2 = 2.

(b) Unit digit

Unit digits of powers of 7 repeat 7, 9, 3, 1 (cycle 4). 95 = 4 × 23 + 3 → 3rd in cycle → 3.

Powers of 3 repeat 3, 9, 7, 1. 58 = 4 × 14 + 2 → 2nd in cycle → 9.

Unit digit of the product = unit digit of 3 × 9 = 27 → 7.

(c) Change of divisor

N = 342k + 47. Since 342 = 18 × 19, the term 342k is divisible by 18. So the remainder is 47 mod 18 = 47 − 36 = 11.

This works only because 18 is a factor of 342.

(d) Divisible by 72

By 8: the last three digits 67y must be divisible by 8. 672 = 8 × 84 → y = 2 (and 670–679 contains no other multiple of 8).

By 9: digit sum 4 + x + 6 + 7 + 2 = 19 + x must be a multiple of 9 → x = 8.

Number = 48672 (= 72 × 676 ✓). So x = 8, y = 2.

(e) Factors of 720

720 = 2⁴ × 3² × 5¹.

Number of factors = (4 + 1)(2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 5 × 3 × 2 = 30.

Sum of factors = (1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16)(1 + 3 + 9)(1 + 5) = 31 × 13 × 6 = 2418.

(f) Remainder of a product

17 → 5, 23 → 11, 29 → 5 (remainders mod 12). 5 × 11 × 5 = 275 = 12 × 22 + 11 → remainder 11.

Quick check with negative remainders: 23 ≡ −1, so 5 × (−1) × 5 = −25 ≡ −25 + 36 = 11 ✓.

(a) 2 (b) 7 (c) 11 (d) x = 8, y = 2 (e) 30 factors, sum 2418 (f) 11

Using the rule for 4 instead of 8 in (d), or testing "divisible by 72" as "divisible by 6 and 12" (not co-prime, so it does not work). In (c), applying the shortcut when the new divisor is not a factor of the old one.

See Unit I, 1.4 and 1.2.

Q3 Percentages: population, price–consumption, election and exam 12 marks

Unit IMultiplying factorSuccessive changePercentage of percentage
  1. The population of a town is 1,25,000. It increases by 8 % in the first year, decreases by 5 % in the second year and increases by 10 % in the third year. Find the population after three years and the net percentage change.
  2. The price of sugar rises by 25 %. By what percentage must a family reduce its consumption so that its expenditure on sugar does not change?
  3. In an election between two candidates, 20 % of the voters did not vote and 10 % of the votes cast were invalid. The winner got 55 % of the valid votes and won by 1296 votes. Find the total number of voters and the votes received by each candidate.
  4. A candidate needs 40 % to pass. He gets 178 marks and fails by 22 marks. Find the maximum marks. Another candidate scores 62 %. By how many marks does she clear the pass mark?

Turn every percentage change into a multiplying factor (+8 % → × 1.08, −5 % → × 0.95) and multiply them. For "keep expenditure the same", price × consumption is constant. For the election, build the chain voters → votes cast → valid votes → margin step by step.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Successive changes

Population = 1,25,000 × 1.08 × 0.95 × 1.10

1,25,000 × 1.08 = 1,35,000 → × 0.95 = 1,28,250 → × 1.10 = 1,41,075.

Net change = (1,41,075 − 1,25,000)/1,25,000 × 100 = 16,075/1,25,000 × 100 = 12.86 % increase (not 8 − 5 + 10 = 13 %).

(b) Price up, consumption down

Expenditure = price × consumption. New price = 1.25 × old, so new consumption = old/1.25 = 0.8 × old → reduce by 20 %.

Formula: reduction = r/(100 + r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = 20 %.

(c) Election

Let total voters = V.

  1. Votes cast = 80 % of V = 0.8V.
  2. Valid votes = 90 % of 0.8V = 0.72V.
  3. Winner 55 %, loser 45 % of valid votes → margin = 10 % of 0.72V = 0.072V.
  4. 0.072V = 1296 → V = 1296/0.072 = 18,000.

Valid votes = 0.72 × 18,000 = 12,960. Winner = 0.55 × 12,960 = 7,128; loser = 0.45 × 12,960 = 5,832. Check: 7,128 − 5,832 = 1,296 ✓.

(d) Pass mark

Pass mark = 178 + 22 = 200 = 40 % of maximum → maximum = 200/0.4 = 500.

Second candidate = 62 % of 500 = 310 → clears the pass mark by 310 − 200 = 110 marks.

(a) 1,41,075; +12.86 % (b) 20 % (c) 18,000 voters; winner 7,128, loser 5,832 (d) 500 marks; 110 marks above the pass mark

Adding percentages in (a) (gives 13 %). Answering 25 % in (b). In (c), taking the percentages of the total voters instead of the valid votes.

See Unit I, 1.5.

Q4 Profit and loss with marked price, discounts, false weights and a chain of sales 10 marks

Unit IMarked priceSuccessive discountsDishonest dealerPercentages
  1. A shopkeeper marks goods 40 % above cost price and allows successive discounts of 10 % and 5 %. Find his profit percentage.
  2. A dealer uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg and sells at 10 % above cost price. Find his actual gain percentage.
  3. By selling an article for ₹720, a man loses 10 %. At what price should he sell it to gain 15 %?
  4. A sells an article to B at a profit of 20 %, B sells it to C at a loss of 10 %, and C pays ₹2,160. What did A pay for it?
  5. Two articles are sold for ₹9,900 each, one at a gain of 10 % and the other at a loss of 10 %. Find the overall gain or loss percentage.

Take CP = 100 whenever no value is given and follow the price through each step with multiplying factors. For false weights compare what the customer pays with what the dealer actually gives.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Mark-up with two discounts

Let CP = ₹100 → MP = ₹140 → after 10 % discount = ₹126 → after 5 % discount = ₹119.70.

Profit = ₹19.70 on ₹100 → 19.7 %.

Single equivalent discount of 10 % and 5 % = 10 + 5 − (10 × 5)/100 = 14.5 %, and 140 × 0.855 = 119.70 ✓.

(b) False weight + mark-up

Let 1 g cost ₹1. The customer pays for 1000 g at 10 % above CP: SP = ₹1,100. The dealer actually gives 900 g, costing him ₹900.

Gain = (1100 − 900)/900 × 100 = 22.22 %.

(c) From a loss to a gain

SP = 90 % of CP → CP = 720/0.9 = ₹800. For a 15 % gain, SP = 1.15 × 800 = ₹920.

(d) Chain of sales

C pays = A's cost × 1.20 × 0.90 = 1.08 × A's cost. A's cost = 2160/1.08 = ₹2,000.

(e) Same SP, equal gain and loss

CP of first = 9900/1.1 = ₹9,000; CP of second = 9900/0.9 = ₹11,000. Total CP = ₹20,000, total SP = ₹19,800.

Loss = ₹200 → 200/20,000 × 100 = 1 % loss.

Shortcut: when SP is the same and the gain % equals the loss % = x, there is always a loss of x²/100 % = 100/100 = 1 %.

(a) 19.7 % profit (b) 22.22 % gain (c) ₹920 (d) ₹2,000 (e) 1 % loss

Adding discounts (15 % instead of 14.5 %). In (b), dividing by 1000 instead of the 900 g the dealer actually spent money on. In (e), saying "no profit, no loss".

See Unit I, 1.6.

Q5 Partnership with withdrawal, a new partner and a working partner's salary 10 marks

Unit ICapital × timeRatioPercentage

A and B start a business with ₹60,000 and ₹40,000. After 4 months A withdraws ₹20,000 and C joins with ₹50,000. A is the working partner and receives 10 % of the profit as salary; the rest is shared in the ratio of capital invested. The profit at the end of the year is ₹1,02,000. Find each partner's share and A's total income from the business.

Profit share ∝ capital × months. Split A's year into two periods because his capital changed. Take the salary out of the profit before sharing the rest.

Show full step-by-step solution

Step 1 — Capital-months

PartnerCapital × monthsTotal
A60,000 × 4 + 40,000 × 85,60,000
B40,000 × 124,80,000
C50,000 × 84,00,000

Ratio = 5,60,000 : 4,80,000 : 4,00,000 = 56 : 48 : 40 = 7 : 6 : 5 (divide by 80,000). Sum of ratio = 18.

Step 2 — Salary first

A's salary = 10 % of 1,02,000 = ₹10,200. Profit left to share = 1,02,000 − 10,200 = ₹91,800.

One part = 91,800/18 = ₹5,100.

A's total = 10,200 + 35,700 = ₹45,900.

Q6 Ratio and proportion: ages, coins, mixture and division of money 10 marks

Unit IRatioCompound ratioMean and fourth proportionalLinear equation
  1. The ratio of the present ages of A and B is 4 : 5. Six years ago it was 2 : 3. Find their present ages. After how many years will the ratio be 5 : 6?
  2. A bag contains 50 p, 25 p and 10 p coins in the ratio 5 : 9 : 4, worth ₹206 in all. Find the number of coins of each type.
  3. 60 litres of a mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3 : 2?
  4. Divide ₹7,000 among A, B and C so that A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5.
  5. Find the mean proportional between 9 and 16, and the fourth proportional to 4, 9 and 12.

Write each quantity as a multiple of x (4x and 5x; 5x, 9x and 4x coins) and form one equation. In a mixture only the added liquid changes — the other stays fixed. For a compound ratio make the common term (B) equal.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Ages

Ages 4x and 5x. (4x − 6)/(5x − 6) = 2/3 → 12x − 18 = 10x − 12 → x = 3. Present ages A = 12, B = 15.

(12 + t)/(15 + t) = 5/6 → 72 + 6t = 75 + 5t → t = 3 years.

(b) Coins

Coins 5x, 9x, 4x. Value in ₹ = 0.50(5x) + 0.25(9x) + 0.10(4x) = 2.5x + 2.25x + 0.4x = 5.15x.

5.15x = 206 → x = 40. Coins: 50 p = 200, 25 p = 360, 10 p = 160.

(c) Mixture

Milk = 7/10 × 60 = 42 L, water = 18 L. Milk stays 42 L. 42/(18 + w) = 3/2 → 18 + w = 28 → w = 10 litres.

(d) Compound ratio

A : B = 2 : 3 = 8 : 12; B : C = 4 : 5 = 12 : 15 (make B = 12 in both). A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15, sum 35.

One part = 7,000/35 = 200. A = ₹1,600, B = ₹2,400, C = ₹3,000.

(e) Proportionals

Mean proportional = √(9 × 16) = 12. Fourth proportional x: 4 : 9 = 12 : x → x = 9 × 12/4 = 27.

(a) 12 and 15 years; after 3 years (b) 200, 360, 160 coins (c) 10 L (d) ₹1,600, ₹2,400, ₹3,000 (e) 12 and 27

In (b), dividing ₹206 in the ratio 5 : 9 : 4 (that is the ratio of the number of coins, not of their value). In (c), changing the milk quantity.

See Unit I, 1.7.

Q7 Averages: overlapping groups, a new member, a batsman and weighted averages 10 marks

Unit ISum = average × countWeighted averageAverage speed
  1. The average of 11 results is 50. The average of the first six is 49 and of the last six is 52. Find the sixth result.
  2. The average age of 39 students is 15 years. When the teacher's age is included, the average rises to 15.5 years. Find the teacher's age.
  3. A batsman scores 87 runs in his 17th innings and raises his average by 3. Find his average after the 17th innings.
  4. Sections A, B and C have 30, 50 and 20 students with average marks 60, 72 and 80. Find the average of all the students.
  5. A car goes from P to Q at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Convert every average into a total (sum = average × number), add or subtract totals, then divide again. Never average averages unless the groups are equal in size.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Overlapping groups

Total of 11 = 550. First six = 294, last six = 312. The 6th result is counted in both: 294 + 312 − 550 = 56.

(b) Teacher included

Total of 39 students = 39 × 15 = 585. Total of 40 people = 40 × 15.5 = 620. Teacher = 620 − 585 = 35 years.

Quick check: the teacher brings 15 years plus 0.5 for each of the 40 people → 15 + 20 = 35 ✓.

(c) Batsman

Let the average after 16 innings be a. Then 16a + 87 = 17(a + 3) → 16a + 87 = 17a + 51 → a = 36. New average = 39.

Shortcut: new average = 87 − 3 × 16 = 39.

(d) Weighted average

Average = (30 × 60 + 50 × 72 + 20 × 80)/(30 + 50 + 20) = (1800 + 3600 + 1600)/100 = 70.

(The simple average of 60, 72 and 80 would be 70.67 — wrong, because the sections are of different sizes.)

(e) Average speed

Let PQ = d. Time = d/60 + d/40 = 5d/120 = d/24. Average speed = 2d ÷ (d/24) = 48 km/h.

Formula for equal distances: 2xy/(x + y) = 2 × 60 × 40/100 = 48.

(a) 56 (b) 35 years (c) 39 (d) 70 (e) 48 km/h

In (e), answering 50 km/h (the simple average of the speeds). In (c), giving the old average (36) instead of the new one.

See Unit I, 1.8.

Q8 Problems on trains: pole, platform, two trains and a walking man 10 marks

Unit IUnit conversionRelative speedLength to cover
  1. A train 240 m long passes a pole in 12 s and a platform in 30 s. Find the speed of the train in km/h and the length of the platform.
  2. Two trains 150 m and 200 m long run at 54 km/h and 72 km/h. How long do they take to cross each other when moving (i) in opposite directions, (ii) in the same direction?
  3. A train 240 m long passes a man walking at 6 km/h in the same direction in 18 s. Find the speed of the train.

Distance to cover = train length (pole or man) or train length + platform length (or the other train's length). Speed = relative speed: add speeds in opposite directions, subtract in the same direction. Convert km/h → m/s by × 5/18.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Pole and platform

Speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h.

Platform: distance in 30 s = 20 × 30 = 600 m = 240 + platform → platform = 360 m.

(b) Two trains

Distance = 150 + 200 = 350 m.

(i) Opposite: relative speed = 54 + 72 = 126 km/h = 126 × 5/18 = 35 m/s → time = 350/35 = 10 s.

(ii) Same direction: relative speed = 72 − 54 = 18 km/h = 5 m/s → time = 350/5 = 70 s.

(c) Train and walking man

Let the train's speed be v km/h. Relative speed = (v − 6) km/h = (v − 6) × 5/18 m/s.

(v − 6) × 5/18 × 18 = 240 → 5(v − 6) = 240 → v − 6 = 48 → v = 54 km/h.

(a) 72 km/h; platform 360 m (b) (i) 10 s (ii) 70 s (c) 54 km/h

Forgetting to add the platform (or the other train) to the distance; mixing m/s with km/h.

Q9 Boats and streams with simultaneous equations, and the late/early problem 10 marks

Unit IUpstream / downstreamLinear equations in 1/u, 1/dTime difference
  1. A boat goes 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hours. It goes 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream in 13 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the stream.
  2. Walking at 5 km/h a student reaches college 10 minutes late; walking at 6 km/h he reaches 5 minutes early. Find the distance to college and the correct time he should take.

(a) Let the upstream speed be u and the downstream speed d. Time = distance/speed gives two equations that are linear in 1/u and 1/d. Then boat = (d + u)/2, stream = (d − u)/2. (b) The two times differ by 10 + 5 = 15 minutes.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Boat and stream

Put p = 1/u and q = 1/d:

30p + 44q = 10 … (1)    40p + 55q = 13 … (2)

(1) × 4: 120p + 176q = 40; (2) × 3: 120p + 165q = 39. Subtract: 11q = 1 → q = 1/11 → d = 11 km/h.

From (1): 30p = 10 − 44/11 = 6 → p = 1/5 → u = 5 km/h.

Boat in still water = (11 + 5)/2 = 8 km/h; stream = (11 − 5)/2 = 3 km/h.

Check (2): 40/5 + 55/11 = 8 + 5 = 13 h ✓.

(b) Late and early

d/5 − d/6 = 15 min = 1/4 h → d/30 = 1/4 → d = 7.5 km.

At 5 km/h: 7.5/5 = 1.5 h = 90 min (10 min late) → correct time = 80 minutes. Check: at 6 km/h, 75 min = 5 min early ✓.

(a) Boat 8 km/h, stream 3 km/h (b) 7.5 km; 80 minutes

In (b), using a difference of 5 minutes instead of 15. In (a), trying to solve directly for u and d (the equations are not linear in u and d).

Unit ILCM methodWages ∝ workM·D·H/W ruleRatio

A, B and C can finish a job in 12, 15 and 20 days respectively.

  1. In how many days can they finish it together? If they are paid ₹4,500 for the job done together, find each one's share.
  2. Instead, A and B work together for 4 days and then leave; C finishes the rest. How long does C take, and how should ₹4,500 now be shared?
  3. 12 men working 8 hours a day finish a piece of work in 10 days. How many days will 16 men working 6 hours a day take to do twice the work?

Q11 Pipes and cisterns: two inlets with a leak, closing a pipe, and tank capacity 10 marks

Unit INegative workLCM methodLinear equation
  1. Pipes A and B fill a tank in 20 min and 30 min; a leak C empties the full tank in 40 min. If all three are open, how long does the tank take to fill? How much later is this than without the leak?
  2. A and B are opened together (no leak). After 6 minutes B is closed. How long does it take in total to fill the tank?
  3. A leak empties a full tank in 8 hours. A tap filling 6 litres per minute is opened, and now the full tank empties in 12 hours. Find the capacity of the tank.

Treat a leak as a worker with negative rate. Use total = LCM(20, 30, 40) = 120 units. In (c), the leak's rate minus the tap's rate equals the new net emptying rate.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) With the leak

Tank = 120 units. A = +6, B = +4, C = −3 units/min. Net = 7 units/min → 120/7 = 17 1/7 min (≈ 17 min 9 s).

Without the leak: 10 units/min → 12 min. Delay = 17 1/7 − 12 = 5 1/7 min.

(b) Closing B

A + B for 6 min = 6 × 10 = 60 units (half the tank). Remaining 60 units by A alone: 60/6 = 10 min. Total = 16 minutes.

(c) Capacity

Let the capacity be V litres. Leak rate = V/480 L/min (8 h = 480 min). Net emptying rate with the tap = V/720 L/min.

V/480 − 6 = V/720 → V(1/480 − 1/720) = 6 → V × (3 − 2)/1440 = 6 → V = 8,640 litres.

Check: leak rate = 18 L/min, minus 6 = 12 L/min → 8640/12 = 720 min = 12 h ✓.

(a) 17 1/7 min; 5 1/7 min later (b) 16 min (c) 8,640 L

Adding the leak's rate instead of subtracting it; in (c) mixing hours and minutes.

Q12 Linear equations: tickets, digits, fractions and three unknowns 10 marks

Unit IForming equationsSubstitution / eliminationThree variables
  1. A cinema sold 500 tickets for ₹63,000. Adult tickets cost ₹150 and child tickets ₹90. How many of each were sold?
  2. The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. When the digits are reversed, the number increases by 27. Find the number.
  3. If 1 is added to both the numerator and denominator of a fraction it becomes 4/5; if 5 is subtracted from both it becomes 1/2. Find the fraction.
  4. Solve: x + y + z = 6, 2x − y + z = 3, x + 2y − z = 2.

Name the unknowns, translate each sentence into one equation, then eliminate. A two-digit number with tens digit t and units digit u is 10t + u. Always check the answer in the original words.

Show full step-by-step solution

(a) Tickets
