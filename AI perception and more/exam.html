E.1 What to expect, and how to play it

  • A proctored, computer-based NPTEL exam at a test centre, normally 3 hours in a morning or afternoon session. The exact date, session and centre are on your hall ticket.
  • Questions are objective: single-answer MCQs and multiple-select questions, often worded like the weekly assignments. Your estimate of about 120 questions means roughly 1.5 minutes per question.
  • The exam is worth 75 of your 100 final marks, and you need at least 30/75 (40/100 in the exam) to qualify for a certificate. See the certificate calculator.
  • Read the on-screen instructions for negative marking and partial credit on the day. Don't assume either.
  • First pass: answer everything you know in under a minute and mark the rest for review. Second pass: the calculations (conv output size, IoU, Q-update, momentum, softmax, mIoU).
  • Multiple-select: judge each option on its own as true or false. Don't stop at the first true one.
  • Eliminate absolutes such as "always", "guarantees", "never" and "regardless of". Most traps in this course's assignments are absolute statements (e.g. "pruning more channels never hurts accuracy").
  • For "why" questions, pick the option that names the mechanism (e.g. "softened outputs reveal relative class similarities"), not a side effect ("it trains faster").
