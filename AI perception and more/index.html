Exam prep built from the course's own lecture slides and your 12 weekly assignments.   1. Learn the week. Read the concept cards. Each one gives the explanation, how it works, a use case and its limitations. Play with every demo, because the exam asks why questions and the demos show the why.
  2. Do that week's assignment questions at the bottom of the page. NPTEL exams often re-use assignment questions with small changes, so read the explanation of every option, not just the right one.
  3. Do the practice MCQs for the week. Your last attempt is saved in this browser.
  4. Take timed mock exams on the mock-exam page. Then use Focus → only ones I got wrong until your weak weeks are above 75%.
  5. The day before the exam, read the one-page revision sheet and its list of exam traps.

Weeks

Week 1 · + Qs

Foundations

AI for drones and aerial perception, AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL, learning paradigms, evaluation metrics (confusion matrix, MSE, R²), linear independence and span.
Week 2 · + Qs

Neurons & Learning Rules

The neuron and its bias, activation functions, Hebbian, perceptron and gradient-descent rules (worked step by step), logic gates and XOR, MLPs and backpropagation.
Week 3 · + Qs

Training That Converges & Generalises

Sigmoid vs tanh, learning rate and momentum, overfitting (Bishop's curve fitting), regularisation, early stopping, and the LS/MLS/FPE/CE/focal loss family.
Week 4 · + Qs · slides not shared

Tensors, Least Squares & RBF Networks

Tensor rank, shape and size; why squared error; Cover's theorem; RBF networks with Gaussian units, XOR in hidden space and exact interpolation.
Week 5 · + Qs

CNN Foundations

Hubel & Wiesel, the Neocognitron, convolution, padding, stride, output-size and parameter maths, pooling, CNN activations, augmentation, PyTorch and transfer learning.
Week 6 · + Qs

Normalisation, Visualisation & Architectures

Batch, Layer, Instance and Group Norm, AdaIN style, receptive fields, saliency and Grad-CAM; LeNet, AlexNet, ZFNet, VGG, GoogLeNet and ResNet in numbers.
Week 7 · + Qs

Sequence Models

Memory-neuron networks for drone system identification, RNNs and BPTT, vanishing gradients, LSTM gates, attention, self-attention and Transformers.
Week 8 · + Qs

Object Detection

IoU and NMS, R-CNN → Fast → Faster R-CNN (RPN, anchors, RoI Pool vs Align), YOLO, SSD, RetinaNet's focal loss, DETR, and why aerial detection is hard.
Week 9 · + Qs

Segmentation

Upsampling and transposed convolution, FCN, SegNet, U-Net, SPP/PSPNet, DeepLab (atrous, ASPP), KiU-Net and DeepMAO, MaskFormer, SAM, mIoU and domain shift.
Week 10 · + Qs

Distillation, Contrastive & Multi-Modal Learning

KL vs MMD, knowledge distillation with temperature, InfoNCE, SimCLR, MoCo and CMC, pixel contrast, RGB–IR fusion and channel exchange, pruning, continual and open-vocabulary learning.
Week 11 · + Qs

Reinforcement Learning & GANs

MDPs, returns, Bellman equations, ε-greedy, Q-learning vs SARSA on a drone grid, DQN, actor–critic, PPO/SAC, sim-to-real; the GAN minimax game, D*, mode collapse and WGAN.
Week 12 · + Qs

Drones in Action

Perimeter defence as spatio-temporal task assignment (DREAM), spiking-network assignment, GNSS-denied navigation, IMU drift and learned inertial odometry, and the SLAM family.

Getting the certificate

Assignment component = 25% of the average of your best 8 of the 12 weekly assignments (out of 25).
Exam component = 75% of your proctored exam score out of 100 (out of 75).
Final score = assignment component + exam component. You are eligible only if the assignment component ≥ 10/25 AND the exam component ≥ 30/75; the final score must also be at least 40.
Certificate levels: 40–59 successfully completed · 60–74 Elite · 75–89 Elite + Silver · ≥ 90 Elite + Gold.

This is NPTEL's standard rule for 12-week courses. Confirm it under "Course layout" or "Announcements" on your course page, because NPTEL can change it for a run.
