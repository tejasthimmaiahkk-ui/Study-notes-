How every page is organised

Each concept is presented as a card with four parts, so nothing is left as a gap:

Concept card

What the idea is, in plain language, built from the basics.
Where it is actually used — so the idea feels real and is easy to recall.
The procedure, algorithm, formula or steps, with a worked example.
Its weaknesses and when not to use it — a favourite exam question.

Around the cards you will find these boxes:

Background you need first. Nothing is assumed from earlier years.
Extra material that deepens understanding. Marked clearly so you know it is optional.
Every step of a solved problem, and the method behind each step.
Questions to solve on your own, with a Show answer button to check your work.
Interactive tools: type your own values and watch the algorithm work.
How examiners ask this topic, and how to write a full-marks answer.

Suggested study plan

  1. Read a section slowly. Do not skip the Foundation boxes: they fill the gaps that usually make later topics feel hard.
  2. Play with the demo. Change the inputs and predict the output before you press the button.
  3. Solve the practice questions on paper, then open the answer. If you got it wrong, re-read the worked example.
  4. Mark the section as understood. Your progress is shown in the sidebar and on this page.
  5. Then practise exam-length problems on each subject's Solved Qs page: 20+ high-weightage, multi-concept questions in the most repeated university patterns, each with the approach and a full step-by-step solution.
  6. Before the exam, use each subject's Revision page: formula sheets, likely questions and a quick self-test. The eye button in the top bar reveals every answer on a page at once for fast revision.
To print a unit as a chapter of a book, use your browser's Print → Save as PDF. Navigation and demos are hidden and every answer is shown.
